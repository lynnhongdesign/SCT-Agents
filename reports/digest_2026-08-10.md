## Top signals this week
- **Indie DTC brand breaks into a major specialty retail channel**: Wilderdog (small adventure-gear dog brand) landed a wholesale partnership putting its products in 40 REI Co-op stores nationwide, with plans to expand further (Outdoor Sports Wire, Aug 3 2026). A concrete example of the offline-retail path a small handmade/DTC seller can eventually pursue.
- **Consolidation is moving "beyond the bowl" into enrichment**: The Farmer's Dog acquired Woof, a wellness brand best known for its refillable "Pupsicle" enrichment device, explicitly to expand beyond fresh food into functional enrichment products (PR Newswire/Pet Age/PetfoodIndustry, Aug 6 2026). Signals where a major nutrition player sees growth — adjacent to toys/enrichment, a category small sellers already compete in.
- **Enrichment products (lick mats, cooling mats) remain durable, high-volume TikTok trends** — not one-off virality but sustained, recurring content with huge cumulative reach (#lickmat ~355.8M views) and multiple creators/brands involved (search + platform-indexed content, ongoing through mid-2026). Both are handmade/small-batch-friendly product categories.
- **Big capital keeps flowing into Food/Treats & Supplements**: Golden Child (fresh dog food) raised $37M (seed + Series A, Redpoint Ventures et al., confirmed via BusinessWire/TechCrunch, Apr 27–28 2026) and Omni (vegan supplements) raised £11M (~$14.5M) Series A for an "Ozempic for dogs" weight-loss product (Green Queen/Vegan Food & Living, June 2026) — these two categories are attracting the most investor conviction right now.
- **Subscription-model caution flag**: BARK Inc.'s Q1 FY2027 revenue fell ~23% YoY ($78.8M vs $102.9M) on a shrinking DTC subscriber base, even as profitability improved (earnings coverage, Aug 7 2026) — a reminder that recurring-box models are under real pressure even at scale.
